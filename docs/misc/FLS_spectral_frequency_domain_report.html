Frequency-domain FLS: from wind/wave spectra to Miner's-rule damage

Spectral fatigue methodology for a monopile-supported offshore wind turbine, reduced to the closed-form, single-DOF architecture already used in engine.py. Supersedes docs/misc/FLS_spectral.md.

§1 Governing equations & linearization

The full integrated system (rotor–nacelle, tower, monopile, soil) is a nonlinear MDOF beam. Frequency-domain analysis requires linearizing about a mean operating point (mean wind speed Ū, mean thrust T̄) so the equation of motion becomes stationary-stochastic-linear:

Msys ẍ(t) + Csys ẋ(t) + Ksys x(t) = Faero(t) + Fhydro(t)
1.1
Msys
= Mstruct + Ahydro(∞) — structural mass + high-frequency hydrodynamic added mass
Csys
= Cstruct + Caero + Chydro — structural + aerodynamic + viscous-drag damping
Ksys
= Kstruct + Kmud — structural stiffness + mudline foundation stiffness

This tool does not run a multi-DOF FE solve — it already collapses the system to a single equivalent DOF (mass meff, stiffness keq, frequency f₀) in _natural_frequency(). Reusing that reduction, the FLS equation of motion becomes:

meff ẍ + ceq ẋ + keq x = fwind(t) + fwave(t)
1.2

— i.e. one new physical quantity is required beyond what already exists in engine.py: the total damping ratio ζ = ceq/(2 meff ω₀), built from structural + soil + aerodynamic contributions (§3).

§3 Load, transfer function & response PSD

Quasi-static moment spectra

Both load paths are converted to a mudline-moment PSD before dynamic amplification is applied — mirroring how _extreme_loads() already produces m_thrust_mnm and m_wave_mnm from a single extreme case, just re-evaluated per frequency.

SM,wind(ω) = (∂T/∂U)² · larm² · |Gind(ω)|² · Suu(ω)
3.1
SM,wave(ω) = |Twave(ω)|² · Sηη(ω)
3.2
∂T/∂U
rotor thrust slope at Ū — data gap: needs a CT(U) curve, not just the single rated thrust_mn now in TURBINE_LIBRARY
Twave(ω)
linearized-Morison moment transfer function, integrated over depth exactly like the existing 40-slice trapezoidal loop in _extreme_loads — same drag linearity assumption, evaluated per ω instead of once
Linearization detail The Morison drag term |u|u is nonlinear; stochastic (Lorentz) linearization replaces it with √(8/π)·σu(z)·u, where σu(z) is the local relative-velocity std. dev. This makes the linearized drag coefficient a function of sea-state severity — technically an iterative fixed point (guess σu → linearize → recompute σu from the resulting spectrum → repeat, typically converges in <5 passes). For a concept-stage single representative sea state, one pass initialized from a rough Rayleigh estimate of orbital velocity is an adequate simplification — flagged as a documented approximation, not silently skipped.

Dynamic amplification & response PSD

DAF²(ω) = 1 / {[1−(ω/ω₀)²]² + (2ζω/ω₀)²}
3.3
Sσ(ω) = DAF²(ω) · [SM,wind(ω) + SM,wave(ω)] / Z²
3.4

ω₀ = 2πf₀ and Z (section modulus) are already computed by _natural_frequency() and _pile_section_properties() — nothing new to derive there, only ζ is new (§1).

§4 Spectral moments → rainflow-equivalent damage

λn = ∫₀∞ ωn Sσ(ω) dω , n = 0,1,2,4
4.1
λn
the n-th spectral moment of the stress PSD — a property of the response spectrum, units stress²·(rad/s)ⁿ. Not a material/fatigue quantity (see notation callout below).
ν₀ = (1/2π)√(λ₂/λ₀) , α = λ₂/√(λ₀λ₄)
4.2
ν₀
mean up-crossing rate [Hz] — replaces the current "one cycle per rotor revolution" assumption entirely
α
irregularity factor — α→1 narrow-band, α→0 broad-band (wind+wave combined is broad-band/bimodal, so plain Rayleigh over-predicts damage; hence Dirlik below)
Notation — λₙ is not the Wöhler exponent Spectral moments and the S-N slope both get called "m" in different corners of the literature, purely by coincidence. Here λₙ (n=0,1,2,4) is exclusively the n-th moment of the stress PSD [stress²·(rad/s)ⁿ] — nothing to do with material fatigue resistance. m, used from eq. 4.4 onward, is exclusively the dimensionless Wöhler/S-N slope exponent, matching engine.py's existing SN_M. They never combine.

Dirlik's closed-form rainflow density

p(Z) = (D₁/Q)e−Z/Q + (D₂Z/R²)e−Z²/2R² + D₃Z e−Z²/2  ,  Z = S/2√λ₀
4.3

D₁, D₂, D₃, Q, R are explicit algebraic functions of λ₀, λ₁, λ₂, λ₄ (Dirlik, 1985) — three densities (exponential + two Rayleigh) fitted to reproduce the true rainflow histogram far better than a single Rayleigh assumption, without ever forming a time series.

E[Sm] = (2√λ₀)m · [D₁QmΓ(m+1) + (D₂Rm+D₃)2m/2Γ(1+m/2)]
4.4

— the closed-form m-th moment of the Dirlik density (exponential and Rayleigh moments are standard Gamma-function results); this is what makes the method non-iterative and cheap.

Damage rate & DNV-RP-C203 bilinear S-N

N(Δσ) = a₁Δσ−m₁  (N≤10⁷)  /  a₂Δσ−m₂  (N>10⁷)
4.5
a₁, a₂
the S-N curve's fatigue strength coefficients (intercepts) for each segment — a₁ = 10log₁₀a₁, i.e. the linear (not log) value of SN_LOG10_A. Units: MPam, so that N (cycles) comes out dimensionless.
D = Σi (ν₀,ᵢ·Ti/a₁)·E[Sm₁]i  ,  utilization = D·DFF
4.6

Today's SN_LOG10_A/SN_M is single-slope. Once the full amplitude distribution is summed (not one equivalent range), the many small cycles below the 10⁷-cycle knee stop being negligible, so the second slope (m₂=5 for DNV-RP-C203 curve D) starts to matter — splitting eq. 4.4's integral at the knee stress is the one genuine new piece of math this adds over a single-slope curve, evaluated numerically (incomplete-gamma, no closed form) the same way the engine already numerically integrates elsewhere. FATIGUE_DESIGN_FACTOR (DFF) plugs straight into 4.6 unchanged.

What this replaces Today: Δσeq = 0.35·σchar (one empirically back-fitted number, recalibrated 4 times against reference designs) and ncycles = rpmavg×seconds/year×life (one cycle per revolution). Eqs. 4.1–4.6 replace both: the stress-range distribution comes from real spectral content, and the cycle count comes from the response's own zero-crossing rate ν₀ — no empirical load factor left to calibrate away.

4.2 DFF: source, and "why DFF not FDF"

DFF = Design Fatigue Factor, DNV-ST-0126 (formerly DNV-OS-J101) — a safety factor applied to calculated damage, sized by inspection access and failure consequence, not a stress/load factor. It's already in engine.py as FATIGUE_DESIGN_FACTOR; eq. 4.6 doesn't introduce anything new here.

Access for inspection/repairFail-safeNon-fail-safe
Accessible12
Not accessible (e.g. below mudline)23

FATIGUE_DESIGN_FACTOR=2.0 corresponds to a typical non-accessible/fail-safe monopile joint. DFF is the term used throughout DNV-ST-0126 and DNV-RP-C203, and the one used consistently in this document and in engine.py's existing constant name.

4.3 D (damage) vs. DEL (damage equivalent load)

Different quantities, not interchangeable: D is dimensionless cumulative damage (Miner's sum, 0→1 at failure); DEL is a stress/moment amplitude — the single constant-range cycle, repeated neq times, causing the same damage. They're related by definition, since Miner's rule gives Σnᵢ·Sᵢm = a·D directly:

DEL = (Σᵢ nᵢSᵢm / neq)1/m = (a·D / neq)1/m
4.7

Here a is shorthand for a₁ above — the standard DEL convention quotes a single equivalent slope m (usually the steeper, first-segment slope), so it implicitly assumes the equivalent range falls in the N≤10⁷ regime. Eq. 4.6 computes D directly, matching engine.py's existing damage/utilization convention. DEL isn't needed unless cross-checking against a supplier-style number (e.g. WISDEM's utilization_constraints.fatigue(M_DEL, N_DEL, ...)) — eq. 4.7 converts either way, with neq picked by convention (1 Hz-year is common).

§5 Implementation plan

Scope: FLS only. ULS/extreme DLCs (13/16/61) are out of scope for this module and not discussed below. Two DLCs drive fatigue damage — DLC12 (power production) and DLC72 (parked/idling) — reflecting that idling governs monopile fatigue as often as production does in practice. Fits into the existing utilization-ratio / notes-list architecture without touching evaluate_monopile's orchestration shape, following the same house conventions as _shell_buckling_check.

5.2 Damping — structural, soil, aerodynamic

One constant value per component, applied identically across every DLC12 wind bin — not a function of Ū. Aerodynamic damping is the one component that changes with operating state, and even then it's a step function (on for DLC12, off for DLC72), not a continuous curve — so it needs no CT(U) slope data at all, unlike the wind-loading spectrum in eq. 3.1.

ComponentDefault ζBasis
Structural (steel)0.5%material damping of a welded tubular tower/monopile — "usually <1%, 0.5% commonly applied"
Soil (foundation, hysteretic)0.5%full-scale-calibrated range ≈0.15–1% (Carswell et al. 2015: 0.17–0.28%; Shirzadeh et al. 2013: 0.25%; rotor-stop-test estimate: ≈1%); radiation damping neglected (excitation frequencies well below 1 Hz)

Dependency note requirements.txt currently declares only streamlit and pandas — no numpy/scipy anywhere in the repo. Eqs. 4.1 and the Dirlik moments in 4.4 are simple enough to implement with math + hand-rolled trapezoidal loops, matching house style; this is a real but avoidable dependency decision, not a forced one.
